# The Human-Copy Doctrine (the whole point)

The user's #1 complaint: these sites end up "looking like every other AI website."
That look comes almost entirely from **generic copy** and empty filler sections.
Your job is to write the actual website text — headlines, about, taglines, service
descriptions, CTAs, review framing — so it sounds like *this specific business run
by a real person*, not a template. Do this in two places:

1. **Write real, specific copy directly into the prompt** (headline options, an
   About paragraph, service-card lines, a tagline). Don't leave them as
   `[write something here]` when you have enough real detail to write it well.
2. **Include an anti-generic directive in the prompt** telling the website builder
   to preserve this voice and avoid the clichés below — because the builder AI will
   otherwise regenerate bland text. (See the "COPY & VOICE RULES" block in the
   template.)

## Specificity is the #1 anti-generic weapon

Generic copy is what's left when you delete every fact. So don't delete the facts —
mine them and put them front and center. Pull and use, verbatim where possible:
- **Real service/menu/product names and prices** ("Precision haircut + beard — $55",
  "Drip sauce", "Half & Half Combo", "$299 flat dealer fee") — not "our services."
- **Real neighborhood/landmark** ("3rd floor in Market Square," "on Peachtree in
  Downtown ATL," "off Tamiami Trail") — not "conveniently located."
- **The owner, the years, the origin** ("family-run since 2009," a real first name if
  approved) — not "our team of professionals."
- **The brand's own phrases** lifted from their IG bio, site, or posts ("Gurnee's
  boldest wings," "over 25 handcrafted sauces") — this is their real voice; match it.
- **Customers' actual words** from reviews as social-proof themes ("one of the best
  cuts of my life," "good vibe and energy") — never invented, always source-labeled.

One concrete, true sentence beats three adjectives. "Fades sharp enough you'll skip
the mirror check on the way out" says more than "high-quality, professional,
top-notch haircuts."

## Match the brand's register

A gritty local wing spot, a luxury B2B coffee exporter, a family barber, and a
neighborhood dog groomer should not sound alike. Before writing, decide the voice
from the sources: gritty/craveable, premium/authoritative, warm/friendly,
no-nonsense/local, playful, etc. Then commit to it — vocabulary, sentence rhythm,
and swagger level all follow. Register is set per business in
`vertical-playbooks.md`; the copy has to actually *sound* like it.

## Ban list — the tells of AI website copy

Do not write (and tell the builder not to write) these unless they're a real,
quoted review:
- "Welcome to [business]" / "Here at [business]"
- "Whether you're looking for X or Y, we've got you covered" / "look no further"
- "nestled in" / "tucked away in the heart of"
- "we pride ourselves on" / "dedicated to providing" / "committed to excellence"
- "top-notch," "state-of-the-art," "cutting-edge," "unparalleled," "second to none,"
  "world-class," "premier," "your one-stop shop," "wide range of"
- "elevate your [X]," "seamless experience," "in today's fast-paced world"
- "passion for [X]," "our team of dedicated professionals," "needs and preferences"
- Fabricated stats and badges ("10,000+ happy customers," "#1 in town") unless verified
- Empty mission-statement blocks and "Our Values" filler that says nothing specific

## Techniques that read as human

- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing. The cut, the sauce, the truck, the street.
- **Have a point of view.** Let one line carry personality or a small claim the
  business would actually make about itself.
- **Vary rhythm.** Mix a short punchy line with a longer one. Fragments are fine.
  Real speech isn't three balanced clauses every time.
- **Write to the real moment.** Game night, wedding Friday, first day the truck broke
  down, the dog's first groom, the buyer sourcing a pallet of green beans. Speak to it.
- **Break the rule of three.** AI stacks trios ("clean, modern, professional") and
  symmetrical triads endlessly. Use two, or four, or an uneven list on purpose.
- **Keep exclamation points rare** and em-dashes from becoming a tic.
- **Localize.** A sentence only true of a business in *that* town beats one that would
  fit any business anywhere.

## Generic → specific (calibration examples)

- Generic: "We offer a wide range of quality grooming services for your beloved pet."
  Better: "Nail trims, de-shedding, breed cuts, and the kind of bath that gets the
  lake smell out — booked by name, done by the same groomer each time."
- Generic: "Welcome to our modern barbershop where quality meets professionalism."
  Better: "Third floor over Market Square. Walk in for a fade, book Jordan if you want
  the cut people keep stopping you about."
- Generic: "We are committed to providing top-notch handyman services you can trust."
  Better: "Fences, drywall, the TV that's been leaning against the wall for a month —
  one call, a real estimate, and it actually gets done."

Write the prompt's copy at the "Better" level. When you genuinely lack the detail to
be specific, use a clearly labeled placeholder AND tell the builder what kind of real
detail belongs there — never paper over the gap with a generic sentence.
